# Decision memo: test a compact co-lead comparison

**Decision:** test an inline side-by-side comparison of the co-lead candidates’ fee, genre fit and first notable work before changing game balance. This is a proposed playtest, not a demonstrated product improvement.

**Evidence from the invented exercise:** 11 of 12 drafts reached two picks, and 8 reached three. The three missing transitions were explicit abandons after pick two. Among the eight completed co-lead selections, elapsed time from the prior pick had a median of 88.5 seconds. Seven drafts ultimately released; two remained unfinished. The generator deliberately constructed this pattern to make the queries auditable. It is not evidence that real players struggle with co-lead selection.

**Hypothesis to test with people:** co-lead comparisons may require scrolling between cards, especially on mobile. A compact view might shorten the comparison without reducing confidence in the selection. Alternative explanations include reading filmographies for enjoyment, real-world interruptions, or lack of interest in the offered choices.

**Next observation:** keep genre, catalog, tier and rules fixed. Ask consenting playtesters to complete the same type of brief; alternate which interface is seen first when practical. Capture starts, incomplete drafts and releases. Record the number reaching two and three picks and the median co-lead interval among those who choose; ask participants what they were doing during long intervals. Check final completion and selection diversity as secondary measures.

**Decision rule:** only adopt the change if observed sessions and participant comments support the same interpretation and there is no evident loss of choice quality. Report the actual sample sizes and conditions; if there are too few observations, leave the decision open. No human observations were supplied with this release.
